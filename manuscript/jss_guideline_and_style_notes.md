## Manuscript preparation points used

- Journal publishes in English.
- Original Article format is appropriate.
- Use British English spelling and punctuation where possible.
- Typical manuscript length should not greatly exceed about 7,500 words, including tables, references, captions, footnotes, and endnotes.
- Include word count with the manuscript.
- Manuscript order: title page, abstract, keywords, main text, acknowledgements/declarations, references, tables with captions, figure captions.
- Abstract should be unstructured and no more than 200 words.
- Use 3 to 6 keywords.
- Main text can follow IMRaD: Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusion.
- Taylor & Francis expects a data availability statement when a data sharing policy applies; restricted data should explain why access is limited.

## Observed JSS article style

- Concise scientific title, often not overly technical.
- Unstructured abstract of one paragraph.
- Keywords immediately after abstract.
- Methods are often split into study design/participants/procedures/statistical analyses.
- Results are compact and table/figure driven.
- Discussion usually starts with a direct summary of the main findings, then compares with literature, explains mechanisms, states practical implications, and ends with limitations.
- Papers avoid overclaiming causality from predictive models.
- Machine-learning papers should make validation design very clear, especially when repeated observations exist.

## Implications for this paper

- The paper should lead with the sport-science problem: future development of young throwers.
- The computer science contribution should be presented as methodological rigour: leakage-aware longitudinal modelling, not just "more algorithms".
- Introduction and Discussion should be enriched by a domain expert with sport-science literature and coaching interpretation.
- Results should emphasise both predictive utility and the limited incremental contribution of isolated physical tests after current score/context.
